🧠 What is Rakshya App?

Rakshya is a personal safety app for women designed to provide fast and reliable emergency assistance. It enables users to send SOS alerts, share live location, and quickly notify trusted contacts during unsafe situations.

👉 Live Demo 👉 GitHub Repo

❗ Why Do We Need a Women Safety App?

With rising safety concerns worldwide, having a reliable emergency SOS app can make a critical difference. Rakshya focuses on reducing response time and ensuring that help can be reached instantly.

⚙️ Key Features of Rakshya

🚨 1. One-Tap Emergency SOS

Instantly send alerts to emergency contacts with a single tap.

📍 2. Live Location Tracking

Share real-time GPS location so others can track your movement during emergencies.

🎤 3. Voice Activation (Future Scope)

Trigger alerts using voice commands when manual access is not possible.

🔐 4. Secure Data Handling

User data and location information are handled with privacy in mind.

👥 Who Can Use Rakshya?

Although designed as a women security app, Rakshya can be used by anyone who needs personal safety support:

  • Students
  • Travelers
  • Night shift workers
  • Anyone needing personal safety support

🛠️ Technologies Used

Rakshya is built using modern technologies:

  • React Native / Expo
  • JavaScript / TypeScript
  • GPS & Location APIs
  • Web technologies (HTML, CSS, JS)

This makes it a scalable cross-platform safety app.

⚡ How Does the SOS Feature Work?

When activated, Rakshya:

  • Sends an emergency alert
  • Shares the user’s live location
  • Notifies pre-selected contacts instantly

This ensures quick response in critical situations.

Q / A

Frequently asked questions about Rakshya

What is Rakshya app?

Rakshya is a personal safety app focused on fast emergency action through SOS alerts, live location sharing, and quick trusted-contact notification.

Why is a women safety app important?

In unsafe situations, response speed matters. Rakshya is designed to reduce response time and make emergency communication immediate.

How does SOS work in Rakshya?

SOS sends an emergency alert, shares live location, and notifies selected contacts in one emergency flow.

Who can use Rakshya?

While designed with women safety in mind, Rakshya can also support students, travelers, night shift workers, and anyone seeking personal safety assistance.

What technologies power the app?

React Native / Expo, JavaScript / TypeScript, GPS & location APIs, plus web technologies like HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

What challenges are involved in development?

Real-time GPS accuracy, mobile permissions, reliable alert delivery, and keeping UI simple in high-stress moments.

What future upgrades are planned?

AI threat detection, emergency-service integration, wearable support, and better voice-trigger activation.

How does voice-triggered SOS work?

Rakshya can listen for a distress phrase you configure and start the emergency workflow when speaking is safer than reaching for the phone—so response can begin even when your hands are not free.

Does Rakshya record audio or video during an emergency?

The product is designed to capture incident evidence automatically when SOS runs, so trusted contacts and your own records have context. Exact behavior depends on device permissions and the build you install.

How does Rakshya protect SOS and incident data?

Sensitive signals and evidence use modern cryptography, including X25519 key exchange, so data stays protected in transit while still supporting reliable emergency delivery.

What is ride monitoring or route deviation?

During trips, Rakshya can watch your live route against an expected path and surface risk when movement looks off-course—useful for rides, commutes, and travel where detours matter.

How do safety check-ins work?

You can schedule check-ins so the app prompts you to confirm you are safe; if you miss one, your trusted contacts can be notified automatically to reduce uncertainty.

Is Rakshya a replacement for calling emergency services (for example 100 or 911)?

No. Rakshya is a companion for alerting people you trust and sharing location—it does not replace police, ambulance, or official emergency lines. Use local emergency numbers when life is in immediate danger.
